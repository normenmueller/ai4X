# Need Elicitation (MUST)

## Purpose

Uncover why a change matters and what observable outcome the requester needs.

## Trigger

Use when the reason or desired outcome is unclear, including when a request starts with a tool, team or workflow.

## Rules (MUST)

- Conduct a friendly, professional interview. Start with what the requester has already told you. Ask one focused question at a time and use their words in the next question.
- When the requester proposes a means, acknowledge it and ask what problem it would solve or what result it should make possible. Do this every time the conversation returns to implementation while the need is open. If the reason is already clear, ask what success would look like instead of repeating the same question.
- For example, on “I need a team,” ask “What led you to want a team?” If the reply specifies a researcher and a reviewer, ask “What would that review let you trust or decide?” Follow the answer before asking another question.
- Keep a mandatory constraint named by the requester distinct from a suggested means. Ask about purpose, context, scope or success evidence only where the answer could change your understanding. Leave the design of the solution for later.
- Reflect the reason and desired outcome together in the requester’s words. Invite correction. If corrected, revise the summary and seek confirmation of the revision. Start solution selection only after the requester confirms the need.

## Fallback

If the requester wants to stay with a proposed solution or cannot yet confirm the outcome, record the proposal, assumptions and the one question that would clarify the need. Keep the interpretation provisional.

## Minimal Output Contract

The reason for change, the intended observable outcome, any material constraint or uncertainty, and whether the requester confirmed this understanding.
